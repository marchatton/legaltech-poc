Our approach

Positioning

CRE diligence work-product automation.

Orbital is not a general legal AI. It turns messy title, survey, and deal document packs into checkable work product with locked evidence.

  • Best fit: US CRE law firm practice groups doing repeatable acquisition/financing diligence.
  • Primary user: junior associate/paralegal doing first-pass extraction and issue spotting.
  • Validator/buyer: senior associate/partner signing off on risk and insurability.

The problem

CRE diligence is time-sensitive, but a single miss can blow up insurability, lender comfort, or value. The bottleneck is not drafting — it is turning a messy pack into artefacts a senior can review without hunting for evidence.

The real competitor is often no decision: teams stick with the manual workflow because the risk of changing tools feels higher than the pain of the status quo.

Approach landscape (what teams do instead)

Status quo (manual)

Juniors read PDFs, track requirements/exceptions in Excel, draft in Word; seniors verify by hunting passages.

Horizontal copilots

ChatGPT/Claude/M365 are great at drafting. The diligence system is still DIY, and auditability is inconsistent.

Legal AI platforms

Harvey/Legora can become workflow builders. Getting to CRE-native outputs can turn into a configuration project.

Contract review tools

Kira/Luminance excel at clause extraction. Title commitments, exception instruments, and survey reconciliation are not the centre of gravity.

"Perfect world" criteria

  • Outputs match your deliverables (structure and tone), not generic summaries.
  • Every material claim is backed by evidence you can click to verify.
  • Missing evidence is explicit (fail-closed), not hand-waved.
  • Exports drop into Word/Excel without copy/paste glue.

Orbital is a different category

Not "chat with PDFs" and not legal research. Orbital is designed around the outputs you already produce and the evidence you must be able to defend.

Work product, not chat

Issues lists, requirement trackers, exception tables, objection/cure asks — in your structure.

Evidence-first UX

Every material claim is backed by locked citations. Click-to-verify instead of "trust the model".

Document reality

Built for messy scans, missing exhibits, and inconsistent formatting. Orbital assumes that reality.

PoC scope (truthfulness)

This demo leads with evidence-first, artefact-first outputs for title + survey quick-start. It does not claim SSO/RBAC, multi-tenant, or deep integrations.

Value

Faster first pass without sacrificing defensibility

Lower review friction — less time hunting for evidence

Reduced miss risk — exceptions and requirements made explicit

Consistency across a practice group

Discovery prompts (keep it interactive)

  • What are your must-have deliverables per deal (exceptions table, requirements tracker, survey memo)?
  • Who does first pass, and who signs off?
  • Where does time actually go: first pass, senior review, or chasing missing docs/exhibits?
  • What have you tried: manual only, ChatGPT/Claude/M365, Harvey/Legora, Kira/Luminance?
  • If we only improved one thing in the next 30 days, what would it be?

Key decisions

ADR-01 Evidence-first: every claim backed by citation IDs and snippet hashes
ADR-02 Fail-closed: unverifiable citations block export by default
ADR-04 Hybrid retrieval: lexical (tsvector) + semantic (pgvector), returns chunk IDs
ADR-05 Durable orchestration: side effects in retryable, idempotent steps
ADR-15 Deterministic chunking: page-bounded, max 20 lines / 1500 chars, overlap 4
ADR-17 Verification is integrity-only (v1): schema + hash, no entailment model
